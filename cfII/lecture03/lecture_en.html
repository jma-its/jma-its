Slide 2

Big question

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

A wall outlet supplies relatively high-voltage AC, while computer chips need low-voltage stable DC. A power adapter or power supply performs the conversion and adds protection.

Think and answer

Why can’t computer chips connect directly to the wall outlet?

Show answer

The voltage and current type are wrong and unsafe for the chips.

Voltage, current, power

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

Voltage is the electrical potential difference, current is the flow of charge, and power tells how quickly electrical energy is delivered. For simple DC calculations, power equals voltage multiplied by current.

Slide 9

Why low stable DC?

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

Computer chips contain extremely small electronic structures. Excess voltage can damage them, while unstable voltage can cause crashes, data errors, or sudden shutdowns.

Think and answer

What can unstable power cause?

Show answer

Crashes, errors, shutdowns, or hardware damage.

Slide 10

Transformer: the big idea

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

A transformer uses a changing magnetic field to change AC voltage. It can raise or lower voltage, but it does not by itself turn AC into DC.

Think and answer

Does a transformer alone produce DC?

Show answer

No. It changes AC voltage but the output is still AC.
