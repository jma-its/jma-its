Part 3 — Motherboard, CPU, and RAM

Slide 10

Motherboard — The Main Board

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

The motherboard is the main circuit board. It carries power and signals and contains connectors, chips, resistors, capacitors, and control circuits. Damage can cause many different symptoms.

Think and answer

Why can motherboard problems be difficult to diagnose?

Show answer

Many different parts depend on the motherboard.

Motherboard: Main Board and Nerves

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

Thin copper paths act like roads and nerves. They carry physical electrical signals between CPU, RAM, storage, ports, network devices, screen, keyboard, and power circuits.

Think and answer

Are data signals on the motherboard physical or magical?

Show answer

Physical electrical signals.

The CPU follows instructions, calculates, compares, and controls work. It is called the brain as a learning analogy, but it does not understand meaning like a human.

CPU: Principle of Work

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

Inside the CPU are huge numbers of tiny transistors. They switch between electrical states very quickly. Switching uses energy and creates heat, so the CPU needs cooling.

Think and answer

Why does the CPU need cooling?

Show answer

Electrical switching creates heat.

RAM is the fast working area for open programs and current data. It is like a desk: useful for work happening now, not permanent storage.

Work now.

RAM: What Does RAM Mean?

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

RAM means Random Access Memory. The CPU can quickly access many different memory locations. More RAM gives more working space when many programs or large data sets are open.

Think and answer

What does more RAM usually provide?

Show answer

More working space.

RAM: Principle of Work

RAM chips hold temporary electrical states and are read and written very frequently. Normal RAM is volatile: its working data disappears when power is removed.

Part 4 — HDD, SSD, cooling, and power

Slide 18

HDD and SSD — Long-Term Storage

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

HDD and SSD both keep the operating system, programs, and files after shutdown. They can have the same physical size and connector, but their internal technology is different.

Think and answer

Do HDD and SSD have the same job?

Show answer

Yes, long-term storage, but they use different technology.

Back to lesson map ↑
Slide 19

Storage: HDD and SSD

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

Storage is different from RAM. RAM supports current work; storage keeps information for later. An old laptop may have a 2.5-inch SATA HDD that can often be replaced by an SSD.

Think and answer

Which is temporary: RAM or storage?

Show answer

RAM.

Back to lesson map ↑
Slide 20

HDD: What Does HDD Mean?

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

HDD means Hard Disk Drive. A motor spins magnetic platters, and a moving head reads or writes data. Mechanical movement makes HDDs slower and sensitive to shock.

Think and answer

Why can shock damage a working HDD?

Show answer

It contains spinning platters and a moving read/write head.

Back to lesson map ↑
Slide 21

SSD: What Does SSD Mean?

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

SSD means Solid State Drive. It uses flash-memory chips and a controller. It has no moving mechanical storage parts and is normally faster, quieter, and more shock-resistant than an HDD.

Think and answer

Does an SSD use spinning magnetic platters?

Show answer

No.

Back to lesson map ↑
Slide 22

Changing from HDD to SSD

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

Replacing an old HDD with a compatible SSD is often a useful upgrade. It can improve startup and loading speed, but compatibility, data backup, cloning or installation, and the condition of the whole computer must be considered.

Think and answer

Does an SSD upgrade repair every possible slow-computer problem?

Show answer

No. RAM, heat, software, CPU, and other problems may also matter.

The CPU may be hidden under the cooling assembly. A heat pipe carries heat, metal fins spread it, and the fan moves air through the fins and vents.

The fan.

Cooling System: Fan, Heat Pipe, Heat Sink

Study the original slide first. Then read the web explanation.

Heat moves from the hot chip into cooler metal and then into moving air. Dust, blocked vents, dried thermal material, or a broken fan can cause thermal throttling or shutdown.

The laptop battery stores chemical energy and supplies electrical energy when the charger is disconnected. Batteries lose capacity with age and may become unsafe if damaged or swollen.
